Order & Sorting

══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

from first principles

This course doesn't present sorting algorithms one after another. It follows a single chain of questions: what it means to put things in order, what that costs, why it costs that much, and what changes when the assumptions change. Each lesson starts from a question the previous one left open and ends by handing a new one on. The algorithms show up along the way, as answers. That's why the lessons below are titled by questions rather than by algorithm names.

## How a lesson works

  • Predict first. Some blocks ask you to commit to an answer before the explanation appears.
  • Checkpoints check themselves. A wrong answer explains the specific misunderstanding behind it.
  • Every claim carries a tag that says how much it's worth: proof, proof sketch, intuition, empirical, heuristic.
  • Each lesson ends with a ledger: what's established, which assumptions are in force, and the open question passed on.
[proof]a guarantee, valid under the stated assumptions.
[proof sketch]the key steps of a proof; details omitted.
[intuition]a reason to expect something; not a guarantee.
[empirical]measured on particular inputs; can change with them.
[heuristic]a design choice that usually helps; no guarantee.

## Lessons

0 What does it mean for things to be in order? Relations, orders, and what exactly the sorting problem asks. [not yet written]
1 How many questions does it take to be sure? Checking an order, finding an extreme, and why knowing an answer differs from proving it. [not yet written]
2 What happens if we repeatedly take the smallest? A first complete strategy, its exact cost, and the information it throws away. [not yet written]
3 How far is an array from sorted, and what does a local fix buy? Measuring disorder, and the price of repairing it one neighbour at a time. [available]
4 Is knowing the order the same as moving into it? Separating the cost of learning from the cost of rearranging. [not yet written]
5 Is there a floor under every comparison-based method? Counting what any algorithm must learn, whatever it does. [not yet written]
6 Can elements travel far without giving up local repair? Long-range moves, and an honest mix of theory and experiment. [not yet written]
7 What if we split the problem by position? Combining solved halves, and where the logarithm comes from. [not yet written]
8 What if we split it by value instead? Randomness, expectation, and a worst case that must be guarded against. [not yet written]
9 How much order do we actually need? When a full sort is more work than the question requires. [not yet written]
10 Can we keep what we have already learned? Maintaining partial knowledge cheaply. [not yet written]
11 Three roads to $n \log n$: what actually differs? Same growth rate, different ideas. [not yet written]
12 What if keys are more than opaque objects? Changing the model, and why "linear time" always comes with conditions. [not yet written]
13 How much uncertainty does the input really carry? Duplicates, presortedness, and how Python's own sort exploits them. [not yet written]
14 What lies beyond a single total order? Partial orders, chains and antichains. [not yet written]
15 What changes outside the idealised machine? Memory hierarchies, disks, and comparisons fixed in advance. [not yet written]
16 What is the shape of the whole? Building the complete mental model. [not yet written]

Read in order: each lesson depends on the ledgers of the ones before it.
