Order and Sorting from First Principles

One line of inquiry in 38 lessons: what it means to put things in order, what that costs, why it costs that much, and what changes when the assumptions change. Algorithms appear as consequences of the questions. That is why every title is a question, and why no algorithm is named before you have built it.

How a lesson works

  1. The title is the question the lesson answers; “Where we are” names the question handed over by the previous lesson.
  2. A prerequisite check links back to exactly what to revisit.
  3. Predict-first blocks ask you to commit to an answer before the explanation unlocks.
  4. Checkpoints gate the next section. You may continue anyway; the skipped checkpoint is recorded as an open gap.
  5. Every non-trivial claim carries a tag saying what kind of claim it is.
  6. Python appears after the idea, the proof and the cost; every visualization runs a faithful port of that Python.
  7. The ledger at the end records what is established, what is assumed, which lenses are in use, and the open question for the next lesson.

The tags

proof
A guarantee, valid under the stated assumptions.
proof sketch
The structure of a proof with details omitted.
empirical
Measured on particular inputs, hardware or implementations.
heuristic
A design choice that usually helps but guarantees nothing.
intuition
A reason to expect something, not a guarantee.
